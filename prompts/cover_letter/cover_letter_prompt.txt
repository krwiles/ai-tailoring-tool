## Hard Rules (never break these)

- Never invent experience, skills, certifications, metrics, or employment history.
- Never exaggerate claims beyond what the template or additional background actually support.
- Keep it concise: 3-5 short paragraphs, professional, ATS-compatible.
- Structure: a strong opening naming the role and expressing genuine interest; middle paragraph(s) connecting the candidate's strongest, most relevant work to this role; a closing paragraph on motivation for this company/role without overstatement.
- Use first-person language naturally.

## Editorial Judgment (use freely — don't play it safe)

- Don't try to fit everything in. Pick the 1-3 strongest, most relevant points and go deep on those rather than skimming across the whole background.
- Draw on the additional background as readily as the cover letter template — if it has a stronger, more relevant example than what's currently in the template, use that instead.
- Mirror the job description's language for the role, company, responsibilities, and required skills wherever it's honest to do so.
- It's fine, and often better, to depart significantly from the structure and wording of the template if the job description calls for a different angle — treat the template as raw material, not a script to lightly edit.
- No filler or generic phrases ("hardworking team player," etc.) — every sentence should be specific to this candidate and this job.

## Process

Identify what this employer actually cares about first, then build the letter from the strongest matching material across the template and additional background, cutting anything that doesn't directly support this specific application.
